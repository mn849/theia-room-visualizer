MGT 409 · Final project

Theia

A source-faithful way to see whether a secondhand listing belongs in your room.

01 · Problem

Marketplaces show objects. Rooms need context.

Buyers save photos, guess scale, cut objects out, and repeat the process across several listings. The friction is highest for unusual furniture and art.

02 · Product

Room → listing → decision

1
Upload one or more room views.
2
Paste a public URL or image.
3
Compare several items together.

03 · Accuracy

Seller dimensions come first.

Structured listing data is parsed deterministically. Windsor stools reading 36 CMT × 58 CMT × 36 CMT become 0.36 m × 0.58 m × 0.36 m. Estimates are labelled when the seller is silent.

04 · Agent

Sol classifies; it does not invent.

listing_agent.py uses GPT-6-Sol through the Responses API for category, pose, and warnings. Dimensions remain outside the model contract. Calls, usage, fallback, and errors are logged.

05 · Interaction

Click the floor corners. Reset when needed.

Session room and item libraries, multi-angle upload, unit-aware room calibration, individual removal, orientation controls, and a click-based planar correction keep the workflow understandable.

06 · Trust

The preview shows what we know.

Source images stay visible. Confidence and provenance are shown. Theia does not log into private marketplaces, bypass access controls, or present a generated visual as proof of fit.

07 · Economics

One model call per new listing.

Dragging, room uploads, and orientation are local. Input text is capped. Runtime logs record token usage when available; missing keys use a zero-token deterministic fallback.

08 · Limits

A photo is not a 3D scan.

Occlusion, lighting, complex cutouts, and unusual room geometry remain imperfect. The next step is phone depth capture, a confirmed cutout editor, uncertainty bands, and structured user testing.

09 · Ask

Help us measure the decision.

Test whether first-time users can compare three listings in under five minutes, whether stated dimensions survive conversion exactly, and whether uncertainty labels improve trust.
